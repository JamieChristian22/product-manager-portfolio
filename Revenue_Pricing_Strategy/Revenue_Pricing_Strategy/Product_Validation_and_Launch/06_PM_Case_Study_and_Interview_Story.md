# 06 — Product manager case study and interview narrative

> Portfolio exercise using designed artifacts. **No real customers, releases or experiment wins are claimed.**

## One-minute executive story

“In my simulated Revenue & Pricing Strategy case study, I framed the customer problem as: Prospective customers cannot easily predict plan value or choose a suitable tier. I proposed **transparent pricing comparison and upgrade journey** and designed a complete validation path—from participant screening and neutral task-based usability testing, to VoC prioritization, a gated cross-functional rollout and a measurement plan. The decision standard was to address high-severity friction before expansion and judge success against pre-defined outcomes and guardrails.”

## Situation, tasks and outcomes — precise framing

**Situation:** Prospective customers cannot easily predict plan value or choose a suitable tier.

**Task:** Design a credible path to validate a feature and decide whether it deserves a staged launch.

**Actions prepared:** research question and usability protocol; observation severity scoring; tagged feedback intake; impact/effort prioritization; scoped feature stories and acceptance criteria; analytics event contract; release gates; rollback triggers; 7/30-day decision reviews.

**Result of the portfolio work:** completed detailed planning and simulated analysis artifacts; **not** proven live conversion impact, user interviews or production deployment.

## Product tradeoffs to explain

1. **Speed vs. certainty:** Release a narrow MVP only after real usability and QA checks, rather than launch many untested features.
2. **Qual vs. quant:** Task-level observation identifies why a flow fails; instrumented experiments estimate whether a change improves population outcomes.
3. **User value vs. risk:** refund requests stable is a launch constraint, not a vanity KPI.
4. **Near-term impact vs. scalability:** Prioritize simplest reversible change until the user/problem signal is better established.

## Stakeholder meeting agenda (45 minutes)

- 0–5: problem, evidence integrity, current known unknowns.
- 5–15: proposed usability research and top risks.
- 15–25: MVP scope, dependencies and owner decisions.
- 25–35: instrumentation, 5% canary and kill-switch.
- 35–45: decision record, unanswered questions and next checkpoints.

## Interview questions and truthful responses

**“Did you actually test with users?”** “I designed a structured research protocol and modeled synthesis, but these portfolio artifacts are simulated. My next step is recruiting consenting participants and reporting actual results.”

**“Did this launch?”** “This is a launch execution plan, not a shipped production feature. It includes readiness criteria, staged exposure, roles, messaging, monitoring and rollback logic.”

**“How would you decide what to build?”** “I would triangulate observed task failures, recurring customer feedback and behavioral telemetry; prioritize by severity, value, reach, confidence and engineering effort; and document explicit tradeoffs.”

**“What would you measure?”** “I would define a primary conversion/adoption metric with an explicit eligibility denominator and monitor guardrails: refund requests stable, no increase in involuntary churn, pricing disclosures approved by legal.”
